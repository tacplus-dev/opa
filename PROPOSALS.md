Why: opa decides entitlements, and a policy bug that silently drops `append_aud` or `extra_claims` mints a token the policy did not mean. - **Restrict the Rego built-ins policies may call** (2026-11-06, nightly scan; promoted 2026-11-10 as #11, done 2026-11-11: `http.send` and `net.lookup_ip_addr` are out of the capabilities and evaluation runs on the RPC's context): `NewPolicyEvaluator` (`internal/opa.go`) prepares every bundle with OPA's default capabilities. A pushed policy can therefore call `http.send` and the DNS built-ins, and from opa's pod at that. Those calls run on `context.Background()` (`Evaluate`), with no deadline, no cancellation and no `trace_id`. The proposal: pass `rego.Capabilities` with the network built-ins removed (or an explicit allowlist), and refuse a bundle that uses them at load. Why: an entitlement or proxy decision should be a pure function of its input. A policy that reaches out stalls kernel's login and proxy paths on a slow host, and its outbound calls would be the only downstream calls opa makes, untraced (tacplus-dev/looper#51). It's new behaviour, because a bundle that uses `http.send` today would be refused, so it needs a design and Jody's approval. Links: `decisions/security-audit-2026-09-12/CONSOLIDATED_REPORT.md` (Medium: "RPC context is discarded", which is a bug and separate, queued in `FIX-high-tier.md`), `raw-fable.md` §4.
